# Finding worthwhile local food

Proposal for Patrik, researched 2026-09-27. Not yet an active playbook or installed integration.

I recommend a dish-first discovery method with separate checks for food quality,
value and practical feasibility. Start with the food and experiences worth seeking,
then find places that deliver them. Use maps to resolve locations and access.
Popularity, obscurity and star ratings should never decide the recommendation alone.

For this playbook, a "hidden gem" means a promising, less-obvious option with a
specific reason to visit. It does not mean objectively undiscovered or guaranteed
excellent. The goal is a good meal at a fair price that fits Patrik's interests.
An excellent famous restaurant can still qualify as a recommendation.

## What the research supports

| Evidence read | Useful practice | Limit carried into the proposal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mark Wiens describes using local blogs and forums, local-language reviews, walking and personal recommendations in his [Migrationology FAQ](https://www.migrationology.com/faqs/). | Search beyond English travel roundups and allow discoveries from people and walking. | Practitioner experience, not a validated prediction rule. Language does not establish a reviewer's residence or independence. |
| Rick Steves' 2025 [Eating Smartly in Europe script](https://classroom.ricksteves.com/videos/eating-smartly-in-europe) favors nearby side streets, short seasonal menus and local custom. | Investigate neighborhood alternatives and what a restaurant specializes in. | European advice cannot make translated menus, tourist locations or year-round menus automatic disqualifiers elsewhere. |
| Jodi Ettenberg's [travel resources](https://www.legalnomads.com/travel-resources/) suggest researching food-market areas and looking near universities for inexpensive meals. | Search by food district, market and everyday eating occasion as well as restaurant name. | These are discovery leads. Customer appearance, a queue or a cheap location does not certify quality or hygiene. |
| Luca and Zervas' [restaurant-review study](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2015.2304) examines manipulation incentives using Yelp-filtered reviews as a proxy. | Look for corroboration and review specificity, including contrary experiences. | Historical Yelp evidence cannot estimate current Moroccan fraud rates or identify a particular review as fake. Negative reviews can also be manipulated. |
| Google's [contribution policy](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7400114?hl=en) prohibits paid, incentivized and manipulated reviews. | Check disclosed incentives and repeated concrete complaints. | A prohibition does not prove that all remaining reviews are genuine. Suspicious patterns warrant lower confidence, not accusations. |

These sources support search practices and caution about evidence. They do not
prove a universally best restaurant-selection algorithm. The workflow below is
my proposed synthesis, and its effort limits and confidence rules need a live pilot.

## Proposed workflow

### 1. Resolve the actual food request

Distinguish "teach me about local food", "find places for this trip" and "where
should I eat now?" A general food question does not require a restaurant shortlist.
For a meal decision, establish the area, local day/time, dietary requirements,
meal spend and acceptable travel effort when these change the answer. Do not infer
current location from the proposed Italy–Morocco itinerary.

Reuse [Patrik's profile](../../../profile/universal-traveler-profile.md), especially
sections 6 and 9. The approximately €40 daily target covers accommodation and other
ordinary spending as well as food. It is not a meal budget. If remaining spend is
unknown, show meal costs without claiming that the whole day fits. Favor everyday
food and local encounters; do not turn dinner into hours of planning.

Classify through the installed research-workflow-v2. A narrow fact check may qualify
for R1; a saved comparative memo usually needs R2. This playbook adds food questions
to that method without replacing its evidence, preflight or release requirements.

### 2. Learn what to look for before choosing where to eat

Build a small food brief from local food writers, cooks, regional guides and venue
menus. Identify dishes, local spellings, ingredients, usual eating occasions and
which preparations or specialties the sources praise. Separate documented local
practice from a writer's preference. Explain unfamiliar dishes in plain language.

For a Marrakech run, start with a verified dish vocabulary and search combinations
of dish, neighborhood and city in English, French and Arabic where useful. Verify
translations and preserve original place names. Example query shapes are
`[dish] [neighborhood] Marrakech`, `[dish] Marrakech prix menu` and the equivalent
local-language wording. Do not invent Arabic spellings or claim a dish is local
merely because a search result says so.

The food brief should answer what to order, how it is normally served, ordering
and payment conventions, and what Patrik should confirm before paying. Source
these tips locally. Do not export one country's tipping or bargaining practice
into another. If an allergy matters, dish names and popularity are insufficient
to verify ingredients or cross-contact arrangements.

### 3. Discover a small, varied candidate set

Use several kinds of lead rather than several copies of one list:

- A named local food writer, specialist guide or credible firsthand account.
- Dish-specific searches and accessible menus or food photographs.
- Maps or another directory for nearby alternatives and exact location.
- Patrik's saved sources, recommendations from people and reported discoveries.

For a researched shortlist, start with roughly six candidates and inspect the
best three or four. This is a proposed effort limit, not a quota or statistical
sample. Broaden only if the usable choices do not meet the request. Include a
less-obvious option when there is a real lead; never fill a "hidden gem" slot with
a random low-review business. Keep famous options eligible.

When Patrik wants help asking locally, suggest a precise question such as
"Where did you last eat this dish, what did you order, and what did you pay?"
Keep recommendations from staff or guides attributed and note known commercial
ties. Do not contact anyone on his behalf without authorization.

### 4. Check the evidence behind each finalist

For each place, record one compact evidence note:

| Field | Required distinction |
| --- | --- |
| Identity | Name, neighborhood, city, exact branch and verified map link when available. Resolve similarly named venues before combining evidence. |
| Food reason | Specific dish and evidence about preparation, taste or consistency. A nice view is a separate benefit. |
| Source basis | Who visited, approximate visit/publication date, dish ordered and what they observed. Identify commercial interests when disclosed; unknown stays unknown. |
| Independence | Trace repeated lists to their original source where possible. A blog embedding a Google review is not another independent diner. |
| Cost | Dated menu or attributed bill, currency, portion basis, drinks and other known charges. Distinguish a current quote from an estimate. |
| Practical fit | Relevant opening time, meal service or sell-out uncertainty, journey effort and booking need. Listed hours do not guarantee service or availability. |
| Counterevidence | Recent concrete complaints, contrary experiences, closures or changes. Separate food complaints from dislike of decor or queues. |
| Coverage | Which reviews/pages were actually accessible, their dates and selection method. An API-selected sample is not a chronological review audit. |

Prefer recent evidence for prices and operations. Seek the last three to six months
of diner evidence when available, but do not impose a universal expiry on food
writing. Older accounts can establish a lead; new ownership or repeated recent
complaints can overturn it. Review what is actually available rather than claiming
a fixed number of reviews was checked.

For a strong recommendation, seek both a specific credible food account and
corroboration from a different origin, plus enough current logistics to act.
A restaurant's menu can corroborate a dish and price, but cannot independently
prove taste. If coverage is sparse, keep a good lead as "promising, lightly
documented" instead of discarding it or inventing support.

### 5. Challenge hype and judge value

Before selecting a favorite, ask what would make it a disappointing choice for
Patrik. Search for evidence of changed quality, unclear bills, pressured ordering,
paid recommendations or a price premium unsupported by the desired experience.
Read mixed and negative accounts in context. Avoid declaring a business a scam
or tourist trap from one allegation.

Compare similar meals and portions nearby when the price is material. A higher
price may buy better ingredients, comfort or a view; explain which benefit has
evidence and whether it matters here. Count travel expense and effort too.

Do not award an "authenticity score". Neither multilingual menus, tourist diners,
polished decor nor popularity proves poor food. Likewise, rough decor, low review
counts and distant locations do not prove a gem. Do not infer customers' residence
or ethnicity from photographs. Treat unusual review patterns as uncertainty,
not an automated fake-review verdict.

### 6. Recommend briefly and stop

Give one first choice and at most two useful alternatives. Compare food fit,
value, experience and journey effort in words, not an uncalibrated numerical score.
Keep quality confidence separate from operational confidence. A well-supported
food recommendation can still have uncertain opening hours.

Use this answer shape:

> **Start with [place, neighborhood].** Order [dish] because [specific reason].
> Expect [supported price or clearly labeled estimate and inclusions].
> [Map link and checked journey information, or the precise location gap.]
> Evidence is [well supported / promising but lightly documented], based on
> [attributed sources]. The main caveat is [concrete issue].
> [One or two alternatives only if they solve a different need.]
> Local food tips: [a few relevant, sourced tips]. Checked [date].

For "eat now", investigate only enough to choose a nearby option and fallback.
For advance research, stop when the shortlist meets the brief, each finalist has
had a contrary-evidence check, and another feasible search is unlikely to change
the choice. An unresolved gap can produce a conditional recommendation; do not
hide it to satisfy the output shape. Recheck volatile facts on the intended day.

### 7. Retain useful knowledge and learn from actual meals

Follow the existing [record workflows](../../../references/assistant-workflows.md).
Store reusable cuisine and ordering advice in the library. Keep a coherent city
food note rather than creating a database row for every restaurant encountered.
Put date-specific trip research under that trip and link shared knowledge.

Preserve suggested, researched and personally tried as distinct states. When
Patrik reports a meal, save the dish, paid price, date and what worked or failed.
One poor dinner can update that venue's note without changing his standing
preferences. This uses existing capture behavior; travel-reflection remains
deferred. No automatic post-meal questionnaire is proposed.

Persist only material that the source permits. In particular, do not build a Git
archive of Maps API responses, reviews or photographs. User-owned observations
and independently sourced findings should remain distinguishable from live
provider content. The API-specific retention question must be settled before
connecting Maps to any automatic save path.

## Tools and integration choice

| Option | What it adds | Tradeoff and recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Existing web research and accessible map pages | Food writing, menus and independent accounts, with direct source links. | Recommended first pilot. Review pages may be inaccessible; disclose missing coverage. A page being accessible does not prove programmatic access elsewhere. |
| Official Google Maps Grounding Lite MCP | Place search and summaries, location context, walking/driving routes and weather. | First integration candidate after the pilot. Useful for practical checks, not a guarantee of good food or complete review access. |
| Places API behind a small wrapper | Structured place fields and a limited review sample. | Defer custom code. Consider only if the official MCP cannot provide a specific required field. |
| Third-party review or Maps MCP | Potentially different search or review access. | No provider selected. Require a demonstrated missing capability, permitted data access, clear costs and maintenance evidence before evaluation or installation. |

Google's current [MCP reference](https://developers.google.com/maps/ai/grounding-lite/reference/mcp)
documents `search_places`, `lookup_weather`, walking/driving `compute_routes` and
place-resolution tools. The listed routing modes are not a public-transport
timetable solution. An AI-generated place summary must stay distinct from source
reviews. No Maps-specific callable tool was found in this session's enabled tool
inventory; that is not a claim about every account or installable provider.

Google's [Grounding Lite guide](https://developers.google.com/maps/ai/grounding-lite),
updated September 24, 2026, documents a Cloud project, enabled service, credentials
and usage billing, plus a demo-key route for prototyping. It also requires source
attribution and sets model/data-use restrictions. Before installation, verify the
chosen host's compatibility, retention behavior, applicable terms and cost limits.
No key, billing setup, installation or live MCP call was made for this proposal.

The separate [Places API reference](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/places/web-service/reference/rest/v1/places)
caps returned reviews at five, ordered by relevance. A wrapper cannot honestly
turn that into a full recent-review analysis. Google also documents caching and
attribution restrictions, a Place ID storage exception and separate EEA conditions
in its [Places policies](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/places/web-service/policies).
Do not assume the two products have interchangeable permissions. This is an
integration constraint to verify, not a legal determination of this account's use.

## Proposed implementation and acceptance

1. After acceptance, add one `references/food-discovery.md` playbook and a narrow
   routing link from `travel-research`. Keep shared evidence and record rules in
   their existing owners. No new food skill, scoring engine or database.
2. Pilot a real Marrakech request with the existing tools. Resolve neighborhood,
   intended meal/time and dietary constraints first. Produce a small shortlist
   plus local food tips, and record actual evidence gaps and research effort.
3. If Maps access is a recurring obstacle, evaluate the official MCP against the
   same request. Compare branch resolution, actionable location/hours information,
   attribution and retrieval effort. Only activate it once the host, data retention,
   credentials and costs are acceptable. Phone/voice/cloud tests stay deferred.
4. Refine the playbook from the pilot and voluntarily reported meals. Do not claim
   improved meal quality from structural checks or synthetic examples alone.

Before adoption, check these cases:

- A viral venue has only copied promotional praise. Keep it a lead, not the top
  recommendation without food evidence.
- A small place has one credible dish-specific account and little else. Preserve
  it as a tentative option with explicit gaps.
- A famous venue has persuasive food evidence and fair value. Popularity alone
  must not reject it.
- An old enthusiastic review conflicts with a recent closure or changed menu.
  Current operational evidence governs the affected claim.
- Only five relevance-selected reviews are available. Report that limitation and
  seek independent evidence rather than claiming exhaustive review analysis.
- Dietary needs or the exact venue remain unresolved. Do not imply these checks
  passed; give the smallest next check or another supported option.
