# Food discovery QC

Completed 2026-09-27 against the playbook at commit `f133e8d`.
The revised [playbook](../../../references/food-discovery.md) is the current guide;
the original proposal remains historical evidence.

The foundation makes sense, but the first version put too much weight on remote
verification and too little on discovery. It could produce a defensible shortlist
while missing the conversations and unexpected meals Patrik wants. This pass
keeps its evidence discipline and adds a practical way to explore.

## What practitioners actually describe

| Source and method | What transfers | What does not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Yulia Dyukova's January 2026 [How I Find Good Food Anywhere](https://thatswhatshehad.substack.com/p/how-i-find-good-food-anywhere) starts with food experts, studies dishes, records what to order and groups options on a map. Reviews are a secondary check. | Seek relevant expertise, attach dishes to places and keep nearby alternatives. Include migrant cuisines in the meaning of local food. | Her personal rating thresholds, large lists and detailed schedules are not validated universal rules or a fit for Patrik's flexible travel. |
| [Mark Wiens](https://migrationology.com/faqs/) combines local-language blogs/forums, walking and recommendations from local contacts. | Use online and on-the-ground discovery together. | An audience-derived network is not automatically available to Patrik. Local-language reviews do not establish local residence or good taste. |
| In this [Robyn Eckhardt interview](https://www.onceuponatiffin.com/post/2017/11/29/a-roving-appetite-an-interview-with-robyn-eckhardt), she describes asking market vendors about ingredients and following conversations into everyday cooking. Her Turkish helped. | Ask specific food questions and follow useful leads. Markets can introduce people and preparations, not merely businesses to rate. | Her language skills, repeated trips and personal invitations cannot be replicated by an online assistant or promised to a traveler. |
| [Julia Kramer and Ryan Sutton](https://www.eater.com/2019/8/23/20828032/restaurant-critics-reviews-food-secrets) describe local networks, repeat visits, ordinary-diner conditions and editorial selection. | Assess the experience behind a recommendation and recognize that positive lists can omit disappointing visits. | Professional tasting schedules and repeat visits are not a traveler requirement. Their methods do not make remote research equivalent to tasting. |
| [Veruska Anconitano](https://thefoodellers.com/tips/how-to-find-restaurants-when-traveling/) starts with Maps, checks recent feedback, asks residents and explores on foot. | Maps can help nearby discovery; current feedback and personal fit matter. | Reject broad claims that local crowds or intuition reliably guarantee quality. Her rating cutoffs are personal heuristics. |

These are identifiable practitioners' accounts, not controlled comparisons.
They disagree on where to start. The defensible shared pattern is relevant
knowledge, specific recommendations, local context and direct experience.
There is no demonstrated universal formula for hidden gems in this source set.

## Findings and repairs

1. **Source selection was underspecified.** Added firsthand detail, city/cuisine
   experience, ordinary versus hosted dining and taste/budget fit. A source can
   be knowledgeable and still recommend an experience Patrik would not enjoy.
2. **The candidate count and recency window were arbitrary.** Removed the six-place
   starting rule and three-to-six-month target. Research depth now follows the
   decision's cost, uncertainty and effort. Volatile facts still need current checks.
3. **Corroboration could favor already-famous places.** One strong firsthand lead
   can justify a low-cost nearby experiment, with its basis disclosed. More costly
   or conflicting decisions merit further verification. Multiple copied lists
   remain one underlying source.
4. **Dish-first research lacked cooking detail.** Added what the source considers
   a good version and the conditions in which it is served. Those judgments stay
   attributed; a photograph cannot prove flavor.
5. **There was no usable on-arrival step.** Added optional market/area exploration,
   questions about specialties, price confirmation, a small initial order where
   appropriate and a fallback. The assistant reasons from Patrik's observations;
   it cannot observe a kitchen remotely.
6. **Feedback did not calibrate recommendations.** Added separation of food
   execution, personal taste, service, value and social atmosphere, with the source
   of the lead when known. One visit does not establish consistency or change a
   standing preference.

These repairs are my synthesis, not claims that every professional uses this
exact process. The strongest countercase is practical: a hungry traveler may
benefit more from a nearby adequate meal than expert-led research. The playbook
retains its short "eat now" path and optional exploration; no interview, source
quota or food tour is mandatory.

## QC disposition

Keep the original safeguards against invented prices, fake certainty, copied
sources and popularity-based judgments. Keep Maps as an optional logistics and
discovery aid. This pass did not revalidate its API details or install anything.

The revised text was read back against the findings. Local links and the R2 record
were checked. A manual scenario walk-through covered a low-evidence cheap stall,
a costly detour, a hosted recommendation, an urgent meal and a famous good venue.
This establishes instruction coverage, not proven meal outcomes or observed agent
behavior. A real Marrakech request remains the useful next pilot.
